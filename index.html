The endeavor

A reliable grid for the
energy transition.

The grid is absorbing solar, wind, and battery storage at record pace. These inverter-based resources don't behave like traditional generators when something goes wrong: during a fault they produce weaker, distorted electrical signatures that can confuse the protection and fault-location systems utilities have relied on for decades.

My research quantifies exactly when and where classical impedance-based fault location breaks down as inverter penetration rises — and when utilities genuinely need traveling-wave technology instead. The goal is evidence grid operators can act on: invest precisely, restore power faster, and keep reliability high while the generation mix changes.

I come to these questions as a practitioner first. Before graduate research, I spent over six years responsible for the operation, maintenance, and protection of a 132/11 kV grid substation serving one of Bangladesh's major industrial facilities — coordinating with national grid authorities, commissioning relays and transformers, and diagnosing live faults from relay event logs. I study fault location the way the engineers who depend on it experience it: through real relays, real measurement chains, and real feeders.

Grid reliability & resilience

Every minute shaved off fault location is a minute of outage avoided. Precise location shortens restoration, cuts crew patrol time across thousands of feeder-miles, and helps catch failing equipment before it fails catastrophically.

Renewable integration

Rigorous, statistically designed evidence on how inverter-based resources change fault signatures — the kind of data that informs protection standards, utility practice, and the safe interconnection of clean energy.

Smarter use of existing assets

Field-data evidence on what the relays already installed on distribution feeders can still deliver — so utilities and ratepayers spend on new fault-location hardware only where it's truly needed.

M.Sc. Thesis · In progress 01

Traveling-Wave versus Impedance-Based Fault Location Under Inverter-Based-Resource Penetration and Realistic Measurement Chains

A statistically designed comparative study

A head-to-head comparison of the grid's two principal fault-location technologies — conducted not under idealized textbook conditions, but with realistic measurement chains (instrument transformers, sensor bandwidth, sampling limits) and rising inverter-based-resource penetration. A design-of-experiments methodology isolates which factors actually drive location error, replacing anecdote with statistically defensible evidence.

Paper · In preparation 02

Can Legacy Distribution Relays Support Modern Fault Location?

A field-data study of impedance-based location and traveling-wave feasibility on an 11 kV industrial feeder

Most distribution utilities cannot afford dedicated fault-location hardware on every feeder. Using field recordings from a real 11 kV industrial feeder, this study evaluates how accurately the protection relays already in service can locate faults with impedance methods — and whether their measurement chain could ever support traveling-wave techniques — giving utilities a data-grounded answer before they spend.

The question

Can the millions of relays already installed on distribution networks be repurposed as fault locators — or is new hardware unavoidable?

Why it matters

Fault location is national
infrastructure work.

/01

Outages carry an enormous economic cost

U.S. Department of Energy–funded studies estimate that power interruptions cost the American economy tens of billions of dollars every year. Faster, more precise fault location directly shortens restoration time on every affected circuit.

/02

The generation mix is changing the physics

Protection and location schemes were designed around synchronous generators with strong, predictable fault currents. Inverter-based solar, wind, and storage respond differently — and the grid's ability to find faults must be re-validated, not assumed.

/03

Precision prevents catastrophes

A fault located to within a tower span — instead of a multi-mile patrol zone — means damaged conductors, failing insulators, and vegetation contacts are found and fixed sooner, reducing repeat faults and ignition risk in fire-prone regions.

/04

Evidence beats vendor claims

Utilities face competing technology claims and finite budgets. Statistically designed comparisons and field-data validation give operators, regulators, and standards bodies a neutral basis for investment decisions that ratepayers ultimately fund.
